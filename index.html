Brands win when their big decisions match their mission.

Consumers reward consistency between what a brand says and what it does. Patagonia kept it, Bud Light lost it, and Liquid Death built it from scratch.

Setting the terms

Authenticity is alignment over time, not origin.

The common definition

Real, original, not in it for the money.

The definition used here

A brand’s stated identity and its behavior line up over time.

Why it matters socially: people use brands to signal who they are. When a brand breaks character, it breaks that signal for its customers too.

Three cases

Kept, lost, built.

Authenticity kept

Every big decision matched the mission, even the ones that cost money.

“We’re in business to save our home planet.”

What it said

  • Mission: save our home planet
  • “Don’t Buy This Jacket” ad (2011)

What it did

  • 1% of sales to grassroots environmental groups for decades; certified B Corp
  • Sept 2022: transferred the roughly $3 billion company. 2% (all voting stock) to the Patagonia Purpose Trust, 98% (non-voting) to the Holdfast Collective
  • About $100M a year in profits not reinvested goes to Holdfast

What it got

  • Loyalty
  • Pricing power
  • Pressure on the rest of the industry
The tax critique supports the thesis. Bloomberg reporters argued the structure avoided about $700M in capital gains taxes. The UC Law Review (“Taxagonia”) calls it legal tax planning and notes he still owed about $17.5M in gift taxes. Either way, the move stayed in character with the mission, and that is why it read as authentic.

Authenticity abandoned

The problem was not who they partnered with. The move connected to nothing the brand had built.

The identity

  • American, blue-collar, sports-first
  • NFL sponsorships, “Real Men of Genius,” “Dilly Dilly”
  • Mainstream marketing never built on social issues

What happened (2023)

  • New marketing VP said the brand needed to move past its “fratty,” “out of touch” image to reach younger drinkers
  • The Dylan Mulvaney partnership was part of that push
  • Anheuser-Busch backed away without standing behind it or explaining it

What it cost

  • Core customers and LGBTQ consumers and advocates both lost trust
  • Broke character twice, trusted by nobody
  • Modelo Especial took #1

Framing. The critique is about brand strategy and how the company handled it. The failure is inconsistency, not inclusion. Anheuser-Busch had a long record of LGBTQ support (see the timeline in Sources), so the careful claim is that Bud Light’s mainstream marketing identity was never built around social issues.

Authenticity manufactured

It is canned water. It has never broken character.

“Murder your thirst.”

The identity

  • Heavy metal branding
  • “Murder your thirst,” “Death to Plastic”
  • Founded 2017 by Mike Cessario, a former ad creative

Why it counts

  • Authenticity can be designed on purpose
  • Customers know it is a bit and buy in anyway
  • Audiences care more about consistency than origin

Scale

  • About 113,000 retail stores
  • Around 5 million TikTok followers
  • $1.4B valuation after a $67M round (March 2024)

Counterargument

Isn’t Liquid Death just clever marketing?

The objection

Liquid Death is just clever marketing. Patagonia is the only real example of authenticity.

The response

Consumers do not audit origin stories. They watch for hypocrisy. Liquid Death’s tone and anti-plastic mission have stayed the same since day one. That is the same test Patagonia passes and Bud Light failed.

The strongest challenge: Death Dust. Critics note every aluminum can has a plastic liner (Plastics Today), and the powdered drink mix packets use non-recyclable packaging with a plastic layer (Print/Dieline), with little public backlash. A possible answer: Liquid Death’s real character is the humor, not environmental purity, so the humor is the part it cannot break.

Stakes

Trust is the one thing a brand cannot buy back.

Consumers vote with their wallets

Brand identity now shapes purchases.

Broken character costs more than sales

Brands that break character lose trust they can’t buy back.

High-stakes moments mean more. Yvon Chouinard turning down hundreds of millions by giving the company away shows what he values.

Authenticity is the decisions a brand makes that line up with who it is.

Back to the hook: Patagonia gave the company away to protect its mission. Bud Light chased a new audience and lost the one it had.
